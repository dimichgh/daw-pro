# Creative / DJ / loop / performance effects — gap survey and the shared-modulation-layer hypothesis

Date: 2026-09-19
Scope: DAW Pro's built-in effect chain today is gain, 4-band EQ, compressor, limiter, reverb, delay (tempo-synced, ping-pong), saturator, gate, chorus, bass enhancer, bit crusher, plus third-party AU hosting (`Sources/DAWEngine/Effects/`, `Sources/DAWCore/Effects.swift`). That is a competent **mixing/corrective** set. It has **no rhythmic, performance, or DJ-lineage creative effects** — the domain a DJ or loop-based producer reaches for constantly and the domain most likely to read as "toy" or "incomplete" to anyone who has used Ableton, Serato, or a Pioneer mixer. This report surveys that domain, tests a specific architectural hypothesis against it, and ends with a ranked build order.

## 1. Rhythmic / gating effects

**Trance gate / step gater.** A step sequencer whose steps set volume (0 or a per-step level) on a tempo-synced grid, chopping a sustained sound into rhythmic stutters. Kilohearts' Trance Gate is described as adding "rhythm to a pad or lead," chops a beat, or adds staccato to an arpeggio; PoiZone's built-in trance gate uses a 16-step pattern synced to host tempo; A1TriggerGate runs "4 beats / 16 steps (or 12 in Triplet mode)" with a **per-step volume fader** [SoundBridge](https://www.soundbridge.io/use-the-trance-gate-effect-to-alter-the-rhythm), [KVR/A1TriggerGate](https://www.kvraudio.com/product/a1triggergate-by-alex-hilton-a1audio), [Kilohearts Trance Gate](https://kilohearts.com/products/trance_gate), [Image-Line PoiZone manual](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio-learning/fl-studio-online-manual/html/plugins/Poizone_TranceGate.htm). Some plugins replace the fixed grid with a Euclidean sequencer (HY-ESG) for less mechanical patterns [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/hy-esg-is-a-free-vstau-trance-gate-plugin-that-hinges-on-a-euclidean-sequencer). Mechanism: ADSR-style envelope per step (attack/release shape the click vs. pump character) driven by a sequencer [SoundBridge](https://www.soundbridge.io/use-the-trance-gate-effect-to-alter-the-rhythm). **Its musical identity depends on tight, grid-locked step edges** — see §9.6 for why that is a distinct engineering requirement from the smooth LFO motion this report's hypothesis otherwise covers for free.

**Tempo-synced tremolo.** Ableton's Auto Filter doubles as this reference: an **LFO** with selectable shape (Sine, Triangle, Saw, Square, Ramp Up, Ramp Down, Wander, S&H) and a **Rate** control in Hz, seconds, or tempo-synced beat divisions, blended against an **envelope follower** via an `Env Mix` knob (0% = pure LFO, 100% = pure envelope), with per-channel `Spin` detune for stereo movement [PCAudioLabs](https://pcaudiolabs.com/how-to-use-the-ableton-live-auto-filter-audio-effect/), [Ableton manual](https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/live-audio-effect-reference/). This LFO+envelope-follower blend, applied to *amplitude* instead of *cutoff*, is a tremolo; applied to *pan*, it's auto-pan; applied to *cutoff*, it's an auto-wah/filter-tremolo — one primitive, three named effects, which is direct evidence for this report's hypothesis (§9). Unlike a trance gate, a sine/triangle tremolo has no hard edges, so it does not carry the same edge-accuracy requirement (§9.6).

**Half-time / double-time.** Not one mechanism but two, confirmed by the Cableguys HalfTime review coverage: a **pitch-drop ("tape") mode**, "automatically set to cut the playback speed in half over the duration of one bar, resulting in the pitch dropping," and a separate **pitch-preserving mode** that "maintain[s] the pitch of the original audio" at the slower tempo, plus a `Smooth` control to retain transient punch or reduce clicks [Samplesound review](https://www.samplesoundmusic.com/blogs/academy/cableguys-halftime-effect-plugin-review), [Cableguys](https://www.cableguys.com/halftime). These are mechanically different effects wearing one name: **pitch-drop half-time is a captured-buffer read at a held constant rate** — the same delay-line/rate family as tape stop (§2, §9.3), just holding at a steady 0.5× instead of ramping to 0 — while **pitch-preserving half-time is a real-time elastic time-stretch**, the signalsmith-stretch family (§8). Initial Audio SlowMo 2 adds a multiband variant so bass can stay slow while highs stay crisp [Initial Audio](https://initialaudio.com/slowmo2-halftime-effect-plugin/).

**Beat repeat / stutter / roll.** Ableton's Beat Repeat: **Interval** (how often it triggers), **Grid** (size of the repeated slice, with a "No Triplets" toggle), **Chance** (0–100%, probability a given Interval/Offset actually fires), **Gate** (total length of the repetition run, in 16ths), **Variation** (randomizes Grid by an amount, on a chosen Trigger) [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/ableton-beat-repeat-plugin), [Equipboard](https://equipboard.com/items/ableton-beat-repeat). This captures a short window of *already-played* audio into a buffer and re-triggers it on a grid — architecturally distinct from anything DAW Pro's effect chain does today (§9.1), and, like trance gate, its retrigger points are edges that need to land tightly on the grid (§9.6).

**Glitch / buffer-shuffle.** Multi-module sequenced effects that apply a *different* processor per step across a pattern: Sugar Bytes Effectrix 2 ships 14 effects (Looper, Grain, Ring, Vinyl, Spectrum, Tonalizer, Crush, Filter, Phaser, Modulation, Delay, Reverb, Level) sequenced over 32 DAW-synced steps [Sugar Bytes](https://sugar-bytes.de/effectrix2), [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/sugar-bytes-effectrix2). Illformed's free Glitch² has 11 modules (reverser, tape-stop, stretcher, crusher, gater, delay, modulator/ring-mod, shuffler, repeater, phaser) each with its own per-step sequencer lane and tempo sync [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/music-tech/in-loving-memory-of-kieran-foster-illformed-glitch2-multi-effects-plugin-made-available-for-free-following-its-creators-passing), [audiopluginsforfree](https://www.audiopluginsforfree.com/glitch-2/). iZotope Stutter Edit uses **Gesture**-triggered envelopes (MIDI-keyed) with a **Gate Width** (repeat rate — lower = faster chop) and **Gate Release** (lower = choppier, higher = smoother) [iZotope](https://s3.amazonaws.com/izotopedownloads/docs/stutteredit2/en/stutter-and-buffer/index.html), [iZotope learn](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/learn-stutter-edit-in-10-minutes.html). Image-Line Gross Beat is the deepest example: a rolling **2-bar buffer**, 36 time-mapping + 36 volume-mapping envelopes, each user-drawn with selectable curve shapes per node (this is the "param curves are the interesting part" the brief asked about — Gross Beat literally *is* a curve editor over a captured buffer) [Image-Line manual](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio-learning/fl-studio-online-manual/html/plugins/Gross%20Beat.htm), [Image-Line envelope panel](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio-learning/fl-studio-online-manual/html/plugins/GrossBeat_EnvelopePanel.htm).

**Granular freeze.** Captures a window and re-synthesizes it as overlapping grains, sustaining a "frozen" texture. AudioThing Frostbite 2 offers four freeze modes (spectral, reverb, convolution, granular) — spectral gives a smooth "glassy" sustain, granular preserves more surface texture/grain [Bedroom Producers Blog](https://bedroomproducersblog.com/2021/03/09/reverb-wars/), [AudioThing](https://www.audiothing.net/effects/frostbite/). Output Portal is the fullest granular-FX product: grain **density, offset, size, count, shape**, pitch (free or scale/chord-snapped), time-stretch, tempo-synced grain delay, plus two graphically-edited looping envelopes that can drive any parameter, and two macro X/Y-pad-controlled slots [Output](https://output.com/blog/granular-synthesis-101-a-portal-exploration), [gearnews](https://www.gearnews.com/output-portal-a-granular-fx-plug-in-with-heaps-of-modulation/).

**Riser builders.** Not really a DSP effect so much as a preset-driven composite: a filtered/pitched noise or tonal sweep gated to the input or to a fixed duration. Kreuzberg Audio UPLIFTER: "one MIDI note, one duration, one preset" produces a riser/build-up/drop/sweep with a custom curve editor; Hyperlift is "a one-knob riser" with a noise wash gated to input plus an 8-module rack [Kreuzberg Audio](https://kreuzberg-audio.com/en/downloads/uplifter/), [EDM Ghost Production](https://edm-ghost-production.com/plugins/hyperlift). This is functionally "noise generator + filter sweep + one-shot envelope," i.e., an assembly of primitives rather than its own DSP block — good evidence for the PRESET control mode (§10). It has no hard edges, so it does not carry the §9.6 edge-accuracy requirement.

## 2. DJ-lineage effects

**The DJ filter (single-knob bipolar HP/LP sweep).** The canonical club-mixer control: one knob, center detent = bypass, one direction sweeps a high-pass up (killing lows), the other sweeps a low-pass down (killing highs) [djlearn.de](https://djlearn.de/en/how-to-use-filters-dj), [raskjaerbo single-knob M4L device](https://maxforlive.com/library/device/2963/single-knob-dj-style-hi-pass-and-low-pass-filter). Why it "feels right": **resonance** at the cutoff is what makes the sweep audible and exciting rather than just "quieter" — "a little making the movement audible, a lot creating a pronounced whistle and sting" — and the DJ TechTools piece on filter design explicitly credits *steepness and resonance* as what separates a characterful filter from a boring one [DJ TechTools](https://djtechtools.com/2011/12/07/filter-vs-eq-which-when-why/), [Perfect Circuit](https://www.perfectcircuit.com/signal/filter-sweeps). It's a performance gesture, not a set-and-forget EQ move — "a good DJ filter has character over and above simply turning the sound down." **This is a purely continuous effect with no discrete edges** — see §9.6/§12 for why that makes it the "good news" contrast case for the block-rate control question.

**Isolator EQ (kill switches).** A structurally different device from a mixing EQ: a steep 3-band filter (bass/mid/treble) where each band has a **full-kill** switch (−∞ dB) and the pots are voiced for dramatic, full-range moves rather than corrective tweaks — "isolators also feature wider frequency ranges, smoother pots... and much more gain per band" [Beats Beyond Borders](https://beatsbeyondborders.com/editorials/tech-review-isolators/), [KLEMENTZ](https://www.klementz.fr/en/dub-mixers-isolator-how-the-variable-3-band-filter-works/). Pioneer's DJM mixers literally let a DJ toggle the same EQ knobs between "EQ mode" and "Isolator mode" [Pioneer forum](https://forums.pioneerdj.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/206014863-EQ-Kill-Switch-on-Rekordbox-DJ).

**Echo-freeze / echo-out.** Set a delay's feedback toward 100% (or a dedicated "freeze" capture) so the repeats sustain indefinitely, then cut the dry signal — the classic "let the echo ring out and disappear behind the next track" DJ move. Serato DJ Pro's extended DJ-FX set includes both a plain **Echo Out** and a **Brake Echo** variant, per Serato's own DJ-FX/Channel-FX documentation (search-summary sourced, not directly fetched in this sprint) [Serato support, DJ-FX & Channel FX list](https://support.serato.com/hc/en-us/articles/10271055029263-DJ-FX-Channel-FX-list) — distinct from the Serato **Hex FX** production-plugin list cited in §6/elsewhere in this section, which is a different product.

**Tape stop / vinyl brake / backspin.** Three related but distinct gestures, all confirmed as separate named effects in **Serato Hex FX's** Braker category (directly fetched): **Back Spin**, **Lo-fi Braker**, **Pitch Braker**, **Roll Out**, **Vinyl Braker**, each with "adjustable deceleration curves and optional pitch/tone modifications" [Serato support, Hex FX Effect List](https://support.serato.com/hc/en-us/articles/11349213839247-Effect-List). Distinctions that matter for design:
- **Tape stop**: pitch/speed sag toward zero, shaped by tape mechanics (motor torque vs. tape inertia); a KVR forum implementation modulates a delay line's length with an **x² curve** rather than linearly, to get the "lazy sag" character; curve shape ranges from a slow droop to "a hard yank into silence" [KVR forum](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=538470), [morphic.com](https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/tape-stop-sound-effects).
- **Vinyl brake**: "a gradual deceleration that mirrors how a real turntable slows when the motor is disengaged, pitching down smoothly over a set time period" — generally a gentler, more linear-then-harder-stop curve than tape stop's sag [vibesdj.io](https://vibesdj.io/glossary/vinyl-simulation).
- **Backspin**: the record is spun backward by hand — a fast reverse-and-pitch-down burst used as an abrupt, attention-grabbing transition, usually muted by the mixer right after [DJing Tips](https://www.djingtips.com/how-to-dj/backspins-stops-and-power-offs/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_spinning).

All three are one-shot ramps whose *engage instant* is a discrete edge (§9.6) even though the ramp itself is continuous once triggered.

**Noise sweep / uplifter.** A DJ-mixer or performance-FX-unit one-touch effect: a noise oscillator swept through a filter, usually gated to the touch/duration of the control (see Riser builders above — same primitive, DJ-mixer framing rather than producer-tool framing).

**Gate-on-exit.** Not a separate effect so much as a *behavior*: when a performer releases a macro/FX-engage button, a well-designed performance effect fades or hard-cuts its tail rather than leaving residue hanging — this is a **release-curve property of the control gesture itself** (see §9/§10, "macro release envelope").

## 3. Pitch/time as a live effect

**Pitch shifter / harmonizer.** A harmonizer mixes one or more pitch-shifted copies with the dry signal to create intervals/harmony; Slate Digital's MetaPitch is cited as giving independent pitch and formant knobs plus a vocoder-style "Robot" mode [Slate Digital](https://slatedigital.com/metapitch-pitch-shifting-plugin/), [LANDR](https://blog.landr.com/formant-shifting/).

**Formant shift.** Manipulates the resonant peaks (formants) *independent of pitch* — shift them down while pitch-shifting up and a vocal stays natural instead of turning "chipmunk"; shift them down without changing pitch for a deeper, more masculine timbre; classic hardware reference is the Eventide H3000-style "Little AlterBoy" combined pitch+formant box [products.zplane.de](https://products.zplane.de/blog/best-formant-shifting-plugins), [LANDR](https://blog.landr.com/formant-shifting/). "Duck the key" (as commonly meant in this context) is the compound move of shifting pitch to match a target key/interval while *dragging formants the opposite direction* to keep the voice's perceived size/character intact — i.e., a **preset that links two params inversely**, not a new DSP block once both pitch-shift and formant-shift exist.

**Turntable warp / wow.** Slow (sub-6 Hz), often slightly randomized pitch modulation simulating an off-center record or worn belt — mechanically identical to a slow LFO (plus S&H jitter) riding a pitch-shift amount. Continuous, not edge-triggered.

**Time-stretch as a live effect.** Real-time elastic tempo-matching of a loop/clip to the project tempo without changing pitch — DAW Pro already has a scoped answer for the underlying algorithm question (see §9/§8).

DAW Pro already picked the pitch/time engine for offline clip stretch: **signalsmith-stretch (MIT)**, chosen in `docs/research/2026-07-05-time-stretch-library-evaluation.md` **[code/prior research]**. It exposes explicit `setFormantFactor`/`setFormantBase` controls (covers formant shift and "duck the key" directly), and documents a `splitComputation` mode specifically meant to make its otherwise "bursty" computation real-time-safe — i.e., the real-time engine question for this whole pitch/time category is "extend an existing, already-licensed, already-integrated-in-spirit library to a new call pattern," not "invent a pitch-shift algorithm." The same doc also flags `HostedAUEffect` + Apple's `AVAudioUnitTimePitch`/`AVAudioUnitVarispeed` as a **zero-new-code fallback** for a cheap real-time preview, since DAW Pro already hosts AudioUnits — relevant here because varispeed-linked pitch/rate is *also* exactly the tape-stop/vinyl-warp/pitch-drop-half-time mechanic.

## 4. Modulation-family FX

**Phaser.** Cascaded all-pass filter stages whose center frequency is swept by an LFO, producing moving notches (a "whooshing" comb-in-phase rather than comb-in-delay character).

**Flanger.** A very short (0–~20 ms) modulated delay mixed with the dry signal plus feedback, producing a jet-swoosh comb filter. DAW Pro's existing **ChorusEffect** is architecturally adjacent: 2-voice LFO-modulated delay lines around a 15 ms center (±10 ms), sine LFO, feedback-free by design **[code, `ChorusEffect.swift`]**. A flanger needs (a) a shorter/lower delay-time range, (b) a feedback path (which the codebase already has a denormal-safe precedent for, in `DelayEffect`'s feedback low-pass **[code, `DelayEffect.swift`]**), and (c) usually one voice, not two. This is an inference from reading both files, not a documented plan — but it is a real, code-grounded "this is a small delta, not a new category" claim.

**Through-zero flanger.** Not a variant of the feedback-delay flanger above — a structurally different mechanism. Classic tape flanging used two tape decks playing the same material, with one deck's speed nudged so its delayed copy could be dragged *through* perfect alignment (zero delay) and out the other side, rather than always lagging behind the dry signal — "much deeper cancellation... impossible to achieve with traditional flanger pedals or simple plugins" [Sweetwater](https://www.sweetwater.com/insync/through-zero-flanging/), [mrblackpedals](https://www.mrblackpedals.com/blogs/straight-jive/6622058-positive-negative-and-through-zero-flanging-probably-more-than-you-ever-wanted-to-know-about-flanging). Implementing this needs a "catch-up and cross the zero point" scheme — a genuinely separate algorithm from a one-directional modulated-delay flanger, not an LFO-depth setting on it.

**Ring modulation vs. frequency shifter — the distinction the brief asked for.** A **pitch shifter** multiplies every frequency component by the same *ratio*, preserving harmonic relationships (100 Hz → 200 Hz and 200 Hz → 400 Hz for a 2× shift). A **frequency shifter** adds the same *fixed Hz offset* to every component, destroying harmonic relationships on purpose (100 Hz → 200 Hz, 200 Hz → 300 Hz for a +100 Hz shift) — in DSP terms it is a **single-sideband modulator**, mathematically the "shift up OR down only" half of what a **ring modulator** does (ring mod = double-sideband: it produces *both* the sum and difference of carrier and input, which is why ring mod sounds metallic/dissonant while a frequency shifter can sound almost like an exotic chorus at small offsets) [McGill CIM](https://cim.mcgill.ca/~clark/nordmodularbook/nm_spectrum_shift.html), [Perfect Circuit](https://www.perfectcircuit.com/signal/frequency-shifters), [KVR Bode shifter thread](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=453329). Musical uses: bells/metallic textures, phaser-and-rotary-adjacent motion, and — at very small Hz offsets mixed with dry — a chorus-like thickening that a pitch shifter cannot produce because it doesn't preserve harmonic ratios. Ableton's Frequency Shifter device exposes exactly this pair of modes (`Shift` vs `Ring`) plus **Coarse/Fine** (Hz offset), **Drive** (distortion, Ring mode only), **Wide** (inverts Spread per channel for stereo), and an **LFO** section (6 waveshapes, Rate free or tempo-synced, Amount) [macProVideo](https://www.macprovideo.com/article/ableton-live/abletons-frequency-shifter-device-what-is-it-and-how-do-i-use-it), [Ableton blog](https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/get-your-freq-on-explore-the-new-shifter-effect-in-live-111/). Implementation-wise, ring modulation is a trivial oscillator-times-signal multiply; a true frequency shifter needs a **Hilbert-transform/quadrature all-pass network** — meaningfully harder DSP [Hilbert-transformer paper](https://www.mikrocontroller.net/attachment/33905/Audio_Hilbert_WAR19.pdf). Both need an **audio-rate** carrier (§9.5), a different constraint from the edge-accuracy question in §9.6.

**Auto-pan.** LFO (or envelope) driving stereo position instead of amplitude — same primitive as tremolo, different target. **[code]** Track-level `pan` already exists as a first-class strip property (`Sources/DAWCore/Model.swift:1206`), and the existing automation-schedule architecture already spans **both** "volume + pan lanes" and "built-in effect-param lanes" in one system (`AutomationSchedule.swift` header comment) **[code]** — meaning the plumbing to target pan with a modulator, not just a drawn curve, already reaches the right place.

**Rotary/Leslie.** Physically: a bass rotor (drum) and a treble horn, mechanically rotating, crossed over around ~800 Hz, each producing Doppler pitch modulation plus amplitude modulation as they spin toward/away from the listener, with independent ramp-up/ramp-down speeds (slow ↔ fast) [Adam Monroe Music](https://adammonroemusic.com/blog/simulating_a_leslie_speaker.html), [Stanford CCRMA Doppler/Leslie paper](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/doppler/dafx02.pdf). DSP shape: 2-band crossover (DAW Pro already has a working 2nd-order Butterworth crossover pattern inside `BassEnhancerEffect` **[code]**) + a small vibrato/vari-delay primitive per band for the Doppler pitch wobble + amplitude modulation, with the rotor/horn speeds themselves being LFO rate targets that ramp rather than snap (mirrors a synth's portamento).

## 5. Stereo / space as performance

**Mid/side width.** M/S-encode, scale the side channel by a width factor, decode — "the Width knob adjusts the M/S balance, narrowing or widening existing stereo content" [production-expert survey](https://www.production-expert.com/production-expert-1/7-stereo-widening-plugins-you-should-check-out-in-2021).

**Mono-maker below a crossover.** Sum to mono only below a chosen frequency, keep the rest stereo — Brainworx's "Mono Maker" is the reference implementation, explicitly "turns stereo to mono under a crossover frequency" [VI-Control discussion](https://vi-control.net/community/threads/plugin-recommendation-for-mono.70548/). **[code]** This is the same crossover-filter shape already built and working inside `BassEnhancerEffect` (`LP(x, fc)` + complementary `HP` at the same corner) **[code]** — the filter primitive exists in-house, just aimed at a mono-sum instead of a harmonic shaper.

**Haas effect / stereo-izer.** A short (1–40 ms) delay on one channel only, cheap and mono-compatible-ish but can cause bass phase cancellation on mono fold-down [production-expert](https://www.production-expert.com/production-expert-1/7-stereo-widening-plugins-you-should-check-out-in-2021). Multiband variants apply a separate Haas delay per band [Apple App Store listing, Multiband Haas Effect](https://apps.apple.com/lb/app/multiband-haas-effect/id1526429747). **[code]** `DelayEffect` already implements exact-sample-offset stereo delay lines with an independently-settable-feel architecture **[code]** — a Haas/stereo-izer effect is close to a constrained, single-channel, short-time instance of the same delay-line machinery already shipped.

**Auto-panning as performance.** Covered in §4 — same LFO-to-pan primitive, listed here because it is *also* squarely a "space as performance" gesture in DJ/loop contexts (classic use: auto-pan a hi-hat or arp to build width without touching the mixdown).

## 7. What makes these feel musical

Consistent findings across every source above:

- **Tempo sync with a real division set, not free Hz, is the default expectation.** Every product surveyed (Beat Repeat's Grid, Auto Filter's Rate, Trance Gate's step grid, Gross Beat's bar-relative envelopes, Timeless's delay sync) defaults to musical divisions. **[code]** DAW Pro already has exactly this vocabulary built and shipping: `NoteDivision` (`Sources/DAWCore/TempoMap.swift`) — 18 cases, whole through 1/32, each with straight/dotted/triplet, serialized as `"1/4"`/`"1/8d"`/`"1/8t"`, already used by the tempo-synced delay's `division` param **[code]**. Every new creative effect's rate/step-length param should reuse this type rather than inventing a parallel one.
- **Curve shape is not a footnote — it's the character.** Gross Beat's whole identity is per-node curve selection on its envelopes; the tape-stop research is explicit that x² vs. linear vs. exponential decay produces "lazy sag" vs. "hard yank" — audibly different effects from the same mechanism.
- **Resonance/steepness is what separates "filter" from "EQ."** The isolator and DJ-filter research both converge on this: character comes from *emphasis at the edge*, not just attenuation past it.
- **Wet/dry and latency matter more here than in corrective FX**, because these effects are engaged and disengaged live, mid-performance — a gate-on-exit / release-curve behavior (§2) is not optional polish, it's what stops a filter sweep or echo-freeze from sounding like a mistake when released.
- **They are performed, not set-and-forget.** "Mapping a physical knob to a filter and performing the sweep in real time often produces more musical results than drawing automation by hand" [beatkitchen.io](https://beatkitchen.io/guides/electronic-music/08-automation-and-movement/). iZotope's whole Stutter Edit UX is gesture-triggered. This is the strongest single argument for the PRESET/MODULATOR control-mode split in §10 — an LLM "playing" a named gesture is a closer analog to how a human uses these effects than an LLM setting a parameter and walking away.
- **Grid-tightness is itself part of the musicality for the gated/repeated subfamily.** This is not a vendor-stated finding — it's this report's own conclusion from §9.6 — but it belongs here because it's as important to "feel" as tempo sync and curve shape: a trance gate or beat-repeat that is even a few milliseconds loose on every edge reads as sloppy specifically *because* machine-tight chopping is the entire appeal of the effect.

## 8. Codebase feasibility grounding (read-only)

Facts used above, gathered without modifying any code:

- **[code]** The effect chain is a closed `EffectDescriptor.Kind` enum with one `...Params` struct per kind, each carried as an additive optional on the descriptor (`Sources/DAWCore/Effects.swift`). Adding a new creative effect follows this existing, well-precedented shape.
- **[code]** Render-thread parameter updates follow a consistent "atomic-POD-publish" convention across `GainEffect`, `ChorusEffect`, `DelayEffect`: a generation-tagged immutable snapshot published via `daw_atomic_ptr`, picked up "at the top of the next quantum" (i.e., once per render callback, **not** per sample). This is efficient and RT-safe for slow-moving knobs but is a load-bearing constraint for anything claiming per-sample modulation or sample-accurate edges (§9.5, §9.6).
- **[code]** `Sources/DAWCore/RenderBlockSize.swift` pins the **offline** render pull at **512 frames** (`RenderBlockSize.offlineFrames`), which at 48 kHz is a **93.75 Hz** control rate (`modulationSamplingHz`), **Nyquist 46.875 Hz**. This number is not a guess or a default — it was deliberately lowered from 4096 frames after a MEASURED regression: at 4096 frames (11.719 Hz, Nyquist 5.86 Hz), a hosted-AU's MIDI controller lane (pitch bend/CC) was found to alias — a commanded 11.72 Hz vibrato bounced as a **static −17.9 ¢ detune** with no vibrato at all, because the modulation rate exceeded the sampling Nyquist. At 512 frames the same test shows all authored rates surviving with the correct rate and small (~7×-attenuated) alias sidebands. The same file also measured the **cost** of each halving (4096 → 1024 → 512 frames): bounce time on an 8-track dense-bend-lane case went 0.449 s → 0.514 s → 0.587 s, a **1.31× total cost**. **This is the single most directly relevant piece of evidence in this whole report for the live-vs-bounce and edge-accuracy questions** (§9.5, §9.6) — it is an *in-house*, already-measured instance of exactly the failure mode this domain risks, not a hypothetical. The same file is explicit that this 512 figure is the **offline bounce path only**; the **live/monitoring path's rate is whatever the audio hardware's buffer size is, is device-dependent and user-changeable, and has never been measured in this codebase** — so a tempo-synced effect built only against the offline number could plausibly sound different live vs. bounced, a gap this report cannot close and flags for `daw-architect`.
- **[code]** `Sources/DAWEngine/MIDISchedule.swift` (`MIDIEventSchedule`/`ScheduledMIDIEvent`) already delivers **sample-accurate discrete events** to the render thread: `sampleTime: Int64` is an absolute per-sample-frame offset from the schedule anchor, not a block index, and the schedule is published via the same `daw_atomic_ptr`/retire-bin convention used for parameter snapshots. `AudioEngine.swift`'s header confirms "sample-accurate multitrack playback" as an existing, proven engine property. This is one natural, already-built precedent for delivering trance-gate/beat-repeat/tape-stop-engage **edges** with sample accuracy, independent of the 512-frame control-rate path — see §9.6. (A second possible resolution — an effect that owns its own internal, transport-position-driven sequencer, the way `ChorusEffect` owns its own LFO phase rather than receiving it from outside — is also viable and is *not* something this report can rule in or out; see §9.6's closing note.)
- **[code]** `AutomationSchedule`/`AutomationParamOverlay` (`Sources/DAWEngine/Automation/AutomationSchedule.swift`) already deliver **pre-computed breakpoint curves** to the render thread for both strip-level volume/pan lanes and built-in effect-param lanes, RT-safe, no allocation, evaluated at **arbitrary sample time** (`value(at t:, points:)`), not block-quantized. This is a *drawn-automation* delivery mechanism, not a free-running oscillator — but because an LFO's value at any future sample is fully deterministic given phase/rate/shape, a tempo-synced LFO *or* a one-shot gesture (tape-stop ramp, riser envelope) could be delivered through this exact same breakpoint-schedule pipeline, generating the breakpoints algorithmically instead of from user drawing, and get **sample-accurate resolution for musical-rate modulation for free** by reusing proven infrastructure instead of building a second delivery path. This does **not** extend to ring-modulation-rate carriers (§9.5) — an audio-rate oscillator needs thousands of breakpoints per second, which is a different scaling regime than a tempo-synced LFO's tens of breakpoints per second, and is a poor fit for a schedule structure meant for sparse, mostly-linear-segment curves.
- **[code]** `ChorusEffect` is a feedback-free, 2-voice, ±10 ms modulated-delay-line effect; `DelayEffect` has a working denormal-safe feedback filter. A flanger needs the delay-line-modulation half of `ChorusEffect` plus the feedback-handling half of `DelayEffect` — both halves already exist and are proven, separately.
- **[code]** `BassEnhancerEffect` contains a working 2nd-order Butterworth crossover (low band via LP, high band via complementary HP at the same corner) — directly reusable for mono-maker and as the band-splitter half of a rotary/Leslie simulation.
- **[code]** Track-level `pan` (`Sources/DAWCore/Model.swift:1206`) is a first-class strip property already reachable by the automation-lane system, alongside effect params — so a modulation layer that targets "any parameter" does not need a separate code path for strip params vs. effect params; the existing automation architecture already spans both.
- **[code]** The generic parameter-automation surface is **numeric-only by construction**: `NoteDivision.nearest(toBeats:)`'s own doc comment states "the generic effect-param surface is numeric-only" and resolves a `paramSlot` — a single scalar index — via `EffectParamSpec.specs(for:)`. This is a real constraint on the two proposed effects in §12 that are *not* single scalars: a per-step array (`steps: [Double]`) and a one-shot trigger (`engage`) — see takeaway 7.
- **[prior research, code-adjacent]** `docs/research/2026-07-05-time-stretch-library-evaluation.md` already selected **signalsmith-stretch (MIT)** for offline clip stretch/pitch, with explicit formant controls and a documented real-time mode (`splitComputation`) not yet exercised. This directly de-risks pitch shifter, harmonizer, formant shift, "duck the key," pitch-preserving half-time, and time-stretch-as-live-effect — the hard algorithmic decision is already made; what remains is real-time integration, not new DSP research.

## 9. The shared-modulation-layer hypothesis — honest assessment

**The hypothesis**: a single modulation layer — tempo-synced LFO with shapes, an envelope follower, a step sequencer, routable to any effect/instrument parameter — covers most of this domain "for free" (gater = LFO→volume, DJ sweep = macro→cutoff, pumping = follower→gain, trance gate = step-seq→volume).

**Where it clearly holds:**
- Trance gate, tremolo, auto-pan, auto-wah/filter-tremolo, sidechain-pumping-style effects, and the *motion* half of a DJ filter sweep are, mechanistically, the same primitive aimed at different targets (volume, pan, cutoff, gain). Serato's own "Shapers" category grouping (Auto Pan, Pumper, Tremolo together, §6) and Cableguys ShaperBox's entire product (§6) are independent commercial confirmation of exactly this claim, not just this report's inference.
- Once a target parameter exists (e.g., ring-mod carrier frequency, rotor speed, frequency-shifter Hz offset), *macro/LFO-driven motion on top of it* is free in the same way.

**Where it needs one addition the brief's framing doesn't name: one-shot gestures, not just periodic modulation.** LFOs and step sequencers are periodic/looping. Riser builds, echo-freeze-then-release, gate-on-exit, and tape-stop/vinyl-brake are **one-shot ramps** (A→B over N beats, then done, optionally re-armable) — a fourth modulator type ("envelope/gesture generator") belongs in the layer alongside LFO/step-sequencer/envelope-follower. This is not a hypothesis failure, it's a scope correction: the layer as literally described (LFO + follower + step-seq) does *not* cover risers/echo-freeze/tape-stop; the layer *plus a one-shot ramp generator* does cover the "motion" half of all three (the "what parameter and curve" half of tape-stop still needs dedicated DSP — see §9.3).

**Where it breaks — and why, specifically:**

1. **Buffer-capture effects (beat repeat, backspin, glitch/shuffle, granular freeze) need a genuinely different signal-flow primitive.** Every effect read in `Sources/DAWEngine/Effects/` **[code]** is either stateless-per-sample (gain, EQ) or a short fixed-capacity line (delay ≤2 s, chorus ≤25 ms). None of them capture and re-emit a *musical-length* window of already-played audio on an independent read head. A modulation layer decides *how a parameter moves*; it cannot retroactively re-trigger audio that already flowed through the chain. This needs a capture ring-buffer with a transport-decoupled read pointer — new infrastructure, full stop. The step-sequencer *half* of Beat Repeat (which slice, how often) can ride the modulation layer once the buffer engine exists; the buffer engine itself cannot.
2. **Granular freeze needs a grain scheduler** (windowing, overlap-add, per-grain pitch/pan) — a different primitive again from the ring-buffer-retrigger case above, even though both are "buffer capture" in casual language. The modulation layer can drive grain *parameters* (density, pitch, offset) once the granular engine exists, exactly as Output Portal's looping envelopes do (§6) — but does not replace the engine.
3. **Tape stop / vinyl brake / turntable warp / pitch-drop half-time modulate the effective playback rate of the source**, not a downstream mixer parameter. Conceptually this is "a one-shot ramp targeting transport read-speed" rather than "an LFO targeting a gain/filter knob" — whether the current engine's sample-clocked transport can tolerate a modulated read rate without its own resampler is an open question for `daw-architect`, not something this report can answer from the effects-chain code alone. Practically tractable today via the KVR-forum delay-line technique (§1) as an **insert effect** rather than a transport-rate change, which sidesteps the transport question entirely — recommended path.
4. **New filter/DSP topologies are orthogonal to the modulation-layer question.** Phaser needs an all-pass stage; a proper resonant multimode filter (for the DJ filter's character) is not the same topology as the existing 4-band EQ's shelf/peak filters; frequency shifter needs a Hilbert-transform network; formant shift needs LPC/cepstral processing (partly answered by signalsmith-stretch, §8); M/S width needs a matrix encode/decode. The modulation layer decides *how* a parameter moves once the parameter (and the DSP block owning it) exists — it does not manufacture missing signal-processing building blocks. This is the single easiest trap in evaluating this hypothesis: concluding "we built the modulation layer, so we got 20 effects" when really the layer gets you the *motion* on top of DSP blocks that still have to be built one at a time.
5. **Control-rate vs. audio-rate is a live architectural fork for carrier synthesis, not a detail.** Scoped precisely: the existing `daw_atomic_ptr`-publish convention **[code]** updates render-thread parameters once per render callback (once per block), not once per sample — call this the "knob-publish path." That is comfortably sufficient for *smooth* modulation: at the measured offline rate of 93.75 Hz (Nyquist 46.875 Hz, §8), the whole musical LFO/tremolo/vibrato band (sub-20 Hz) sits well under Nyquist with images attenuated ~7× — this is not a guess, it is the exact conclusion `RenderBlockSize.swift`'s own measurement reached for an analogous case (hosted-AU controller-lane modulation). Separately, the **breakpoint-schedule path** (§8) evaluates at arbitrary sample time, so a *baked, deterministic* LFO or one-shot ramp delivered that way gets exact per-sample resolution "for free," sidestepping the knob-publish path's coarseness entirely for anything that can be precomputed. Neither path, however, can synthesize a **carrier running at audio rate (20 Hz–5+ kHz)** the way ring modulation or through-zero flanging need: the knob-publish path is capped by its once-per-block cadence, and the breakpoint-schedule path would need thousands of breakpoints per second to represent an audio-rate waveform — both a poor fit and outside what that structure was built for. Audio-rate carriers need their own per-sample oscillator primitive, evaluated inside the effect's own `process()` call, independent of both existing delivery paths. This should be a named, deliberate decision before implementation starts.
6. **Edge accuracy is a separate, distinct constraint from both of the above — and it is the one this report initially underweighted.** A trance-gate step, a beat-repeat retrigger, a stutter/roll chop boundary, and a tape-stop/vinyl-brake `engage` instant are not "smooth modulation, just faster" — they are **discrete edges** whose timing precision matters on its own terms. On the knob-publish path (§9.5), a scalar update can only take effect at the top of the next 512-frame block, i.e. **0–10.667 ms late relative to its ideal musical position, averaging ~5.3 ms**, computed directly from the measured 512-frame/48 kHz block period (`512/48000 = 0.010667 s`, matching `RenderBlockSize.swift`'s own 93.75 Hz figure). At 128 BPM a sixteenth note is `60/128/4 = 0.11719 s = 117.19 ms`, so the average lateness is **~4.5%** of a sixteenth note and the worst case **~9.1%** — and, because it depends on where the ideal edge falls relative to the nearest block boundary, it is *inconsistent* edge-to-edge within the same pattern, not a fixed, learnable offset. This is my own assessment, not an assumption carried over from elsewhere: **a trance gate's entire musical identity is machine-tight, grid-locked chopping** — genre-defining precisely because it reads as more-than-human-tight — so a few milliseconds of inconsistent lateness on every edge is not a rounding error, it undermines the one property the effect exists to deliver. **This is not fixed by raising the modulation layer's evaluation rate** — that only shrinks, never eliminates, the quantization, and each further halving of the block size has a *measured* bounce-time cost (§8: 4096→1024→512 frames cost 1.31× total bounce time for that one path alone), so pushing the rate further is not free even before the quantization is fully eliminated. **It is fixed by scheduling the edges themselves as discrete, sample-accurate events**, reusing the already-proven `ScheduledMIDIEvent`/`MIDIEventSchedule` pattern (§8) instead of the knob-publish path. The *shape between* edges (an attack/release curve, a decay tail) can still ride the coarser knob-publish or breakpoint-schedule path — only the edge instant itself needs sample accuracy. Items that land on the edge-accurate side: **trance gate/step gater, beat repeat/stutter/roll, glitch/buffer-shuffle step boundaries, tape stop/vinyl brake/backspin's `engage` instant, and pitch-drop half-time's onset.** Items that do *not*: the DJ filter (§9.6a below), tremolo/auto-pan (continuous shapes, no edges), phaser/flanger/rotary (continuous), riser builders (a single smooth ramp, no internal edges).

   There is a second possible resolution worth naming for `daw-architect` alongside the event-schedule fix above, because this report's reading was of the effect chain's *external* parameter-delivery paths, not of whether an effect can be self-contained: a `stepGate` effect whose sequencer logic lives **inside its own `process()` call**, reading transport/beat position directly and computing its own gate state per sample — the same shape `ChorusEffect` already uses for its own LFO phase, which is never published from outside at all — would have per-sample edge accuracy with **no publish path in the picture**, sidestepping the whole knob-publish-vs-event-schedule question. Whether built-in effects' `process()` calls currently receive (or could receive) transport/beat position is something this report did not read and cannot answer — it is the fact that decides between the two resolutions, and is a concrete question for `daw-architect` to settle before committing to either.

   - **9.6a — the DJ filter, specifically, as requested.** Its musical quality depends entirely on **continuous** sweeping — a hand or macro moving `position` smoothly over hundreds of milliseconds to several seconds. There is no edge to quantize. At 93.75 Hz the sweep is updated roughly every 10.7 ms even during the fastest deliberate flick (~100 ms), i.e. ~9 update points across the fastest realistic gesture — likely inaudible as steps *provided* the filter's internal cutoff/Q coefficients are interpolated within a block rather than held constant for the whole 512 frames (several existing effects in this codebase already do per-sample interpolation internally for their own LFOs, e.g. `ChorusEffect`'s per-sample phase accumulator — but whether a *new* DJ filter's coefficient smoothing follows that same internal pattern is an implementation detail for whoever builds it, not something this report can confirm without reading code that doesn't exist yet). Conclusion: **the DJ filter is fine at the measured 93.75 Hz control rate**, and is the correct contrast case to the edge-accurate family in the same table.
- **Live-vs-bounce consistency**: no product surveyed in §6 publishes this level of internal detail, so this report cannot compare DAW Pro's approach against a named competitor's. The most useful thing found is `RenderBlockSize.swift`'s own history (§8): it is a first-hand, measured account of this exact failure class occurring once already in this codebase (aliased controller-lane modulation on the offline path) and being fixed by lowering the block size — which is itself evidence that **block-rate-only delivery is a real, recurring risk in this codebase specifically**, not a theoretical concern imported from outside. The same file is explicit that the **live path's rate has never been measured** — so before shipping any edge-accurate creative effect, the live (hardware-buffer) path needs its own measurement pass, not an assumption that it behaves like the 512-frame offline figure.

**Bottom line**: the hypothesis is correct for the *smooth* amplitude/pan/cutoff-motion family (roughly a third of the domain, but the cheapest, highest-payoff third — see ranked shortlist) and for adding expressive motion on top of any *already-existing* parameter. It is not a shortcut for buffer-capture effects, genuinely new filter/shifter topologies, audio-rate carriers, or transport-rate effects — those need dedicated DSP work whether or not the modulation layer exists. And even within the "smooth motion" family, the *edge-triggered* rhythmic effects (trance gate, beat repeat, stutter/roll) need a second delivery mechanism — sample-accurate event scheduling (or a self-contained, transport-aware effect, per the note above) — layered alongside or instead of the modulation layer, not automatically solved by it. The layer should be scoped and roadmapped as **infrastructure for the effects that need it**, not as a blanket substitute for the rest of the domain.

## 11. Ranked shortlist — highest musical-impact-per-unit-of-work

1. **Trance gate / step gater** — cheapest DSP of any item on this list (pure step-seq → volume), explicitly named genre-defining effect. **Revised from the original draft**: its full musical quality also requires either the already-proven `MIDIEventSchedule` sample-accurate event pattern for its step edges, or a self-contained transport-aware sequencer inside its own `process()` (§9.6) — not just the block-rate modulation layer. Real, but bounded, additional scope, not new DSP invention. Still the best effect to ship first because it proves both delivery mechanisms (knob-publish for shape, edge-accurate scheduling for transitions) end-to-end at once.
2. **Tempo-synced tremolo (+ auto-pan as the same primitive)** — same infra as #1's smooth half, no edge-scheduling need at all (§9.6); ships essentially for free alongside it.
3. **DJ filter (single-knob resonant HP/LP sweep)** — the single highest brand-identity item in this whole domain and named explicitly in the brief; needs one real new filter topology plus careful curve/resonance tuning, but nothing exotic, and — per §9.6a — its performance motion is confirmed fine at the measured control rate with no edge-accuracy risk.
4. **Flanger** — smallest DSP delta of any *missing filter/delay-family* effect, because both halves it needs (`ChorusEffect`'s modulated delay line, `DelayEffect`'s feedback handling) already exist and are proven in this codebase; continuous, no edges.
5. **Ring modulation** — cheap DSP (oscillator × signal) for a dramatic, currently entirely-missing sound; **requires its own audio-rate oscillator primitive** (§9.5) — neither existing delivery path covers it, so budget for that explicitly rather than assuming it rides the modulation layer.
6. **Tape stop / vinyl brake** — contained, well-precedented DSP scope (variable-rate delay-line resampler, §9.3); one of the two DJ-lineage effects named explicitly in the brief; its `engage` is a discrete edge (small-scope — one trigger, not a repeating grid) and its ramp is a good candidate for the breakpoint-schedule path (§8) once triggered.
7. **Mono-maker (crossover)** — cheapest item in the whole stereo/space category because the crossover filter it needs already exists inside `BassEnhancerEffect`; high payoff for anyone mixing/mastering for club or streaming loudness; continuous, no edges.
8. **Beat repeat / stutter** — the highest-complexity item on this list (needs the new capture-ring-buffer primitive, §9.1, **and** sample-accurate retrigger scheduling, §9.6), but it is *the* effect most associated with "used in loops, by DJs" in the original brief, and deferring it indefinitely would leave the single most-requested rhythmic effect unshipped; recommend it get its own `daw-architect` design spike rather than being bundled into a "quick creative FX" sprint.

## Actionable takeaways

1. **ROADMAP — ship the modulation-layer infrastructure as its own milestone item before any single creative effect**, because 5+ of the top-8 shortlist items (trance gate's smooth half, tremolo, auto-pan, DJ-filter motion, flanger) are near-free once it exists, and every future creative effect compounds on top of it. Scope: LFO (shapes: sine/tri/saw/square/S&H, minimum) + step sequencer + envelope follower + **a one-shot ramp/gesture generator** (the missing fourth type identified in §9) + macro-release-envelope behavior, routable to both strip params (volume/pan — already reachable per §8) and effect params (already reachable via the existing per-kind `EffectParamSpec` pattern).
2. **ARCHITECTURE — resolve the control-rate-vs-audio-rate question (§9.5) before building.** The existing "publish at top of quantum" convention is right for trance-gate-shape/tremolo/filter-sweep rates but cannot drive ring-mod/through-zero-flanger-style audio-rate carriers without a second, per-sample oscillator primitive alongside it. Decide explicitly; don't discover it mid-implementation.
3. **ARCHITECTURE — treat edge-accurate scheduling (§9.6) as a separate, named requirement from the modulation layer, not a rate setting on it.** Trance gate, beat repeat, stutter/roll, glitch/buffer-shuffle step boundaries, and tape-stop/vinyl-brake/backspin's `engage` instant all need their discrete transitions delivered with sample accuracy. Two candidate resolutions, either of which `daw-architect` should evaluate rather than this report picking one: (a) reuse the already-proven `ScheduledMIDIEvent`/`MIDIEventSchedule` pattern (`Sources/DAWEngine/MIDISchedule.swift`) to deliver edges as discrete events from outside the effect; or (b) give the affected effects their own internal, transport-position-aware sequencer inside `process()` — the shape `ChorusEffect` already uses for its own LFO phase — which needs no external publish path at all. Which is cheaper depends on whether effects can currently read transport/beat position inside `process()`, a fact this report did not verify. Either way, don't assume a faster knob-publish rate would fix it — it wouldn't (see the computed ~4.5%/9.1% sixteenth-note slop in §9.6).
4. **ARCHITECTURE — scope the buffer-capture primitive (§9.1) as its own `daw-architect` design spike**, parallel in spirit to the existing time-stretch evaluation doc. This blocks beat repeat, backspin, and glitch/shuffle — the highest-recognition items in the "used in loops, by DJs" brief — and is architecturally unlike anything in `Sources/DAWEngine/Effects/` today (every existing effect is stateless-per-sample or a short fixed-capacity line, never a transport-decoupled read head over a musical-length buffer).
5. **ARCHITECTURE — extend, don't re-litigate, the pitch/time engine choice.** Pitch shifter, harmonizer, formant shift/"duck the key," turntable warp, pitch-preserving half-time/double-time, and time-stretch-as-live-effect should all point at `docs/research/2026-07-05-time-stretch-library-evaluation.md`'s signalsmith-stretch pick (real-time via `splitComputation`) with the already-noted `HostedAUEffect` + `AVAudioUnitTimePitch`/`Varispeed` fallback for a cheap interim real-time preview. This changes their complexity rating from "new DSP research" to "real-time integration of an already-licensed, already-scoped library." Pitch-drop half-time and tape stop instead belong with the buffer/rate family (takeaway 4), not this one — they are mechanically a held or ramped playback rate, not a stretch algorithm.
6. **ARCHITECTURE — flag two near-zero-cost reuse opportunities explicitly for the build plan**: (a) flanger as a delta on `ChorusEffect` (shorter delay range) + `DelayEffect` (feedback/denormal handling), both proven in-house; (b) mono-maker and the rotary/Leslie crossover as reuses of `BassEnhancerEffect`'s existing 2nd-order Butterworth crossover filter.
7. **ARCHITECTURE — the generic `fx.setParam` surface is numeric/scalar-only (§8) and does not cover two shapes this domain needs regularly: patterns (a trance gate's `steps` array) and one-shot triggers (tape stop's `engage`).** Resolve both explicitly rather than discovering the gap per-effect: patterns need either N indexed scalar params or a new pattern-typed param kind; triggers need a command/event path. This is precisely why the PRESET tier in takeaway 8 is not optional polish — it is the only clean way to expose a one-shot gesture through MCP at all, given the current param surface's shape.
8. **MCP/control-protocol — ship a PRESET tier above raw params/modulator-routing**, e.g. `fx.applyGesture(kind: "tapeStop" | "filterRiser" | "tranceGate" | "echoFreeze", ...)`, sitting above `fx.setParam` and a modulator-routing tool. §7 and §10's control-mode column both point the same direction: for a non-musician narrating intent in natural language, a single named-gesture call that bundles the right params + modulator + defaults is dramatically more likely to produce a musically correct result than the LLM guessing 5–8 raw DSP values — this is the concrete mechanism that makes the "AI-native, full control via MCP" pitch actually land for this specific effect domain. Every shipped effect still needs its own control-protocol command + MCP tool + test per the standing per-project convention; the PRESET tier is additive on top, not a replacement.
9. **Ship order** matching the ranked shortlist (§11): modulation-layer infra (+ its edge-scheduling companion, takeaway 3) → trance gate (proves both mechanisms end-to-end) → tremolo/auto-pan (free alongside, no edges) → flanger (cheap DSP delta) → DJ filter (the highest-identity new topology, confirmed edge-free) → ring modulation (needs its own audio-rate oscillator, budget accordingly) → tape stop/vinyl brake (contained, iconic, PRESET-first, one-edge scope) → mono-maker (free via `BassEnhancerEffect` reuse) → beat repeat (its own spike, not bundled — needs both the buffer primitive and edge scheduling).
10. **Before shipping any edge-accurate effect, measure the live (hardware-buffer) control rate** — `RenderBlockSize.swift` is explicit that only the 512-frame offline figure has been measured, and the live path's rate is device-dependent and unmeasured in this codebase. Shipping a tempo-synced gate/repeat effect that was only validated on the offline bounce risks a live-vs-bounce mismatch of exactly the kind `RenderBlockSize.swift`'s own history warns about.
11. **Docs hygiene**: `docs/research/README.md`'s standing-questions list does not currently track this domain; recommend the caller add an entry to its "Answered" section pointing at this report, and open two new standing questions — the buffer-capture-primitive spike (takeaway 4) and the live-block-rate measurement (takeaway 10).
