### F2 😖 The documented knob for pad count cannot reach the documented default limit

**Trying to:** step 2→3a. Do the plain chop the skill's own recipe prescribes:
`clip_slice_to_sampler {clipId, mode: "transients"}` at defaults.

**Skill says:**
> `sensitivity` (0..1, default 0.5): low finds only strong hits (clean drums), high
> finds many (ghost notes, legato phrases).
> `maxSlices` | refusal ceiling, default 32, hard cap 96

and, in the "run this first" paragraph, that `count` is what tells you in advance
"whether the chop you are about to commit will produce 4 pads or 40."

**Expected:** on a clean, machine-tight 2-bar drum break — the single most canonical
input this skill can receive — the default settings work, and if they don't, dropping
`sensitivity` toward 0 gets me under 32.

**What happened.** `clip.detectTransients` at default 0.5 → `count: 43`. So I swept
the *entire* documented range before committing anything:

| sensitivity | 0 | 0.05 | 0.1 | 0.2 | 0.25 | 0.3 | 0.4 | 0.5 | 0.7 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `count` | 35 | 35 | 35 | 36 | 37 | 39 | 40 | 43 | 45 | 47 |

**The floor of the whole knob is 35 — above the default `maxSlices` of 32.** There is
no value of `sensitivity` at which the default chop succeeds on this file. The knob's
usable travel is 35→47, i.e. it only ever adds ghost notes; it cannot subtract real
ones. So the confirming call:

```
node daw.mjs clip.sliceToSampler '{"clipId":"F92D801F-…","mode":"transients"}'
```
```
"chopping clip F92D801F-7E16-4E96-A9DE-167E4EB2F294 would make 36 pads, over the
 maxSlices limit of 32 — nothing was changed. Raise maxSlices (hard cap 96), lower
 sensitivity, raise minSliceSeconds, or trim the clip to a shorter passage. Slices
 are never silently dropped, because a chop missing its last bar still looks like a
 successful chop"
```
(exit 1)

✅ **That error message is the best thing I have hit all session** and I want it on the
record as a positive. It names the actual number, the actual limit, states plainly that
nothing changed, lists *four* concrete remedies, and explains *why* the refusal exists.
It is a better teacher than the paragraph in the skill that describes it.

**But one of its four remedies is a dead end on this input** — "lower sensitivity"
cannot get below 35 — and the skill presents `sensitivity` as *the* pad-count knob
while never saying the detector has a hard floor. An agent that trusts the doc will
sweep sensitivity, watch it not work, and have no idea whether it is holding the tool
wrong.

**What I did next:** used the third remedy, `minSliceSeconds`, which the skill does
document as "raise it for fewer, longer pads from busy material". `0.25` (an 8th note
at 90 BPM is 0.333 s) → **15 pads**. That worked first try. But nothing in the skill
lets me predict 0.25 → 15; I picked it from musical arithmetic about the source, not
from the document.

**Suggested fix:** say in §3a that on dense percussive material `sensitivity` bottoms
out well above 32 and `minSliceSeconds` is the real pad-count control — and give the
one-line rule of thumb, e.g. "pads ≈ duration / minSliceSeconds".

### F3 📄 `count` from `detectTransients` is not the pad count, and the skill says it is

### F4 🐛 A discarded head containing the break's downbeat produced NO warning

**Skill says (§3a), verbatim:**
> **`headSeconds` is always reported** and a large discarded head is warned about, so a
> missed downbeat can never vanish quietly. If it looks big, re-run with
> `includeHead: true`.

**Call:**
```
node daw.mjs clip.sliceToSampler '{"clipId":"F92D801F-…","mode":"transients","minSliceSeconds":0.25}'
```
**Result:**
```json
{"sliceCount": 15, "headSeconds": 0.3333106575963719, "warnings": [],
 "trackId": "15193B5A-8369-4ABD-8E44-B9AE953C66CC",
 "createdTrackId": "15193B5A-8369-4ABD-8E44-B9AE953C66CC",
 "midiClipId": "C1C36015-56C4-4776-A09B-05F7BD6076DC",
 "sampleRate": 44100, "frameCount": 250634}
```

`warnings` is **empty**. The discarded head is 0.3333 s — at 90 BPM that is *exactly one
eighth note*, 5.9 % of the file, and pad 1 (`index: 0`) starts at `beat 0.49996`. So
everything before the timeline's beat 0.5 is gone from the pad map, and the first thing
in that region is the file's very first onset at `sourceSeconds 0.0218` — **the
break's downbeat**. That is precisely the outcome the sentence above promises "can
never vanish quietly," and it vanished quietly.

Whether 0.333 s counts as "large" is undefined anywhere I can see. Either the threshold
is wrong for musical material (a head that is a whole beat-subdivision long is
categorically a missed downbeat, regardless of absolute seconds), or the doc's promise
is overstated. From a user's chair I cannot tell which, and that is the problem: I only
noticed because I was reading every field of the payload for this log.

**What I did next:** took the doc's advice literally — it "looks big" to me — undid the
chop and re-ran with `includeHead: true`. Details below.

**Suggested fix:** warn on a head that exceeds some musical fraction (a 16th note at the
project tempo?) rather than, or as well as, an absolute second count. And if there IS a
threshold, state it in the table.

### F6 🐛 `instrument.describeSampler` silently omits the zone array unless you pass `offset`/`limit`

**Trying to:** step 7's instruction, verbatim: "**Capture ids first:**
`instrument_describe_sampler {trackId, offset, limit}` is the purpose-built read — zone
count, each zone's ranges and per-zone fields WITH its `id`, and `effectiveGlobals` —
without the ~290 KB `project_snapshot` cost."

**Call 1 (the obvious one — a read tool, one target, no pagination asked for):**
```
node daw.mjs instrument.describeSampler '{"trackId":"8E66AB0A-3B91-4466-A5B5-E27A22F50FAC"}'
```
**Result: exit 0, 2046 bytes, top-level keys** —
`['attack','effectiveGlobals','gain','kind','oneShot','release','trackId','warnings','zoneSummary']`

**There is no `zones` key. There is also nothing that says there could have been one.**
No `truncated`, no `returnedZones`, no `zonesOmitted`, no `limit` echoed back. The
payload reads as a complete, successful description of the instrument — it even
correctly reports `zoneSummary.count: 16`, so it is visibly *aware* of 16 zones while
handing back none of them.

**Call 2 (adding the two params the skill lists but does not mark required):**
```
node daw.mjs instrument.describeSampler '{"trackId":"8E66AB0A-…","offset":0,"limit":16}'
```
**Result: exit 0, and now** `zones` (16 entries, each with `id`), plus `returnedZones`,
`offset`, `limit` and `truncated` appear.

So `offset`/`limit` are effectively **required** to make the tool do the job the skill
says it exists for, and their absence produces a plausible, well-formed, wrong answer
rather than an error or a flag.

**Why I am calling this a 🐛 and not just a doc gap:** this codebase already knows the
right pattern and states it in the shared wire reference for `clip_add_midi` —
> "The reply then carries `notesOmitted: true` and `noteCount` instead of the array, so
> it can never be confused with a clip that genuinely has no notes."

That exact discipline is missing here, in a reply whose entire purpose is to hand back
ids. An agent that ran call 1, saw no ids, and believed the response would conclude the
purpose-built reader can't give ids — and fall back to `project_snapshot`, which is the
~290 KB trap the same paragraph exists to steer you away from. The failure routes you
into the hazard the tool was built to avoid.

**Cost to me:** one extra call plus a keys-dump to be sure I wasn't misreading. Cheap
only because I was already dumping every payload for this log.

**Suggested fix:** either default `limit` to something non-zero, or add
`zonesOmitted: true` + a one-line note naming `offset`/`limit`. And in the skill, mark
them **required**, not incidental.

### ✅ `effectiveGlobals` is the best-designed response in the whole surface

Unprompted, call 1 told me:
```
"release: the stored global (0.05) is NOT what you'll hear — every zone overrides it;
 the engine actually applies 0.01. See effectiveGlobals.release."
"attack: the stored global (0.001) is NOT what you'll hear — every zone overrides it;

with `globalIsInert: true`, `zonesOverriding: 16`, `effectiveValues: [0.01]` and a note

I would have spent a cycle later "shortening the release for a tight choke" by setting
a global that does nothing — which §5 of the skill actively invites me to think about.
Whoever wrote this: it worked on a live user.

### ✅ The A/B measurement — the property the whole workflow rests on — holds

Recipe step 7 says to play source and reconstruction against each other and that "they
should be indistinguishable." With no ears, `render.measureLoudness` over the same
window with each muted in turn is the stand-in. Window `{fromBeat: 0,
durationSeconds: 6.0}` both times (from beat 0 deliberately — the wire reference warns a
`fromBeat`-windowed render drops MIDI note-ons before the window start):

| | source clip alone | 16-pad reconstruction alone | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| integrated LUFS | **−17.999** | **−18.345** | −0.35 LU |
| true peak dBTP | −1.494 | −1.568 | −0.07 dB |
| max short-term LUFS | −17.663 | −17.988 | −0.33 |
| max momentary LUFS | −14.686 | −15.048 | −0.36 |
| loudness range LU | 1.776 | 1.837 | +0.06 |

**0.35 LU apart on integrated loudness, 0.07 dB on true peak.** That is below the ~1 LU
figure usually quoted as audible, so the doc's "indistinguishable" claim is supported —
by measurement, not by my ears, which do not exist. It is not *bit*-identical, and I
would not expect it to be: each pad gets a 0 ms attack and a 10 ms release, and
boundaries moved by up to 132 frames (3.0 ms) of snapping, both of which shave a hair
off the tails. Worth stating in the skill, because "indistinguishable" invites an agent
to file a bug when it measures a third of a dB.

This is the deliverable-1 evidence: **the chop happened and the generated MIDI clip
plays it back at the source's level.**

### F8 😖 `minSliceSeconds` merging keeps the FIRST onset of a cluster, not the strongest

**Discovered by:** reading the chop's own returned data against the transient list
before writing any new pattern — trying to work out which pad is the snare, since I
cannot listen.

`minSliceSeconds: 0.25` was the remedy F2 pushed me to. It works, but here is what it
did to three of my sixteen pads. Comparing the pre-chop transient list against the
committed slice spans (both already quoted above):

| pad | starts at beat | its own `strength` | strong onset swallowed inside it | that onset's strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40 | 1.8906 | **0.016** | beat 2.0005 | 0.344 |
| 45 | 4.3906 | **0.017** | beat 4.4996 | 0.364 |
| 46 | 4.8499 | **0.089** | beat 5.0003 | **0.992** |

Pad 46 is the case that matters: it begins on a ghost note and the break's second-loudest
hit in the whole file — almost certainly a backbeat snare, `strength 0.992` — sits
**0.10 s inside it**. So triggering pad 46 gives you a ghost, a tenth of a second of
air, and *then* the snare. On the grid that reads as a flam, or as a late snare, and it
is invisible unless you diff the two payloads by hand. `strength` on the slice reports
the boundary onset (0.089), not the loudest content, so the response actively
under-reports what the pad contains.

I only caught this because I was hunting for which pads to call "snare" without ears.
An agent that raised `minSliceSeconds` on the tool's own advice (see F2's error text:
"raise minSliceSeconds") and went straight to writing a pattern would have a
mysteriously flamming backbeat and no thread to pull.

**Suggested fix:** when merging a cluster, keep the strongest onset as the boundary, not
the earliest — or at minimum report a `containsStrongerOnsetAt`/`peakStrength` per slice
so it is visible. The tool already returns `strength`; making it the *span's* peak
rather than the *boundary's* would fix the reporting half for free.

**What I did next:** treated 40 and 45 as ghost/filler pads (they are), and deliberately
did **not** use pad 46 as a backbeat in the flip — I used pads 38 and 50 (`strength`
0.998 and 1.000, both landing on metric backbeats in the source) instead. Working around
it cost me nothing musically because I had 16 pads to choose from. On a 4-pad chop it
would have cost the beat.

⚠️ **Stated plainly: every "this pad is the snare / this is the kick" judgement below is
inference from onset `strength` plus metric position in the source. I have not heard any
of it.**

That is *precisely* the piece of knowledge I needed, stated twice, at the exact moment I

**Working call:**

node daw.mjs track.setInstrument '{"trackId":"8E66AB0A-…","sampler":{"zonePatches":[
   {"id":"A052C73B-…","minPitch":24,"maxPitch":36},
   {"id":"221A405C-…","minPitch":51,"maxPitch":63,"reverse":true},
   {"id":"CAD779A1-…","chokeGroup":1},  … 13 more …
]}}'
```
exit 0, and an independent re-read confirms all sixteen patches landed **and every zone
kept its id** (merge-by-id, not a wholesale replace):
```
pitchCoverage {min: 24, max: 63}, count 16
36 → 24..36                     (16-Levels DOWN: the head/kick as a pitched sub)
37..50 → chokeGroup 1 each      (drum chops cut each other; 36 and 51 ring through)
51 → 51..63, reverse: true      (16-Levels UP + backwards riser)
```
⚠️ Note I patched by `chokeGroup`, not `group` — §5's warning that reusing `group`

**The one thing left unaddressed:** the skill says sending `zonePatches` via
`track_set_instrument` is "REJECTED at the MCP schema." Fine at the MCP layer — but §7

that turns out to be the only one available there. One sentence — "over the raw control
protocol, `track.setInstrument {sampler: {zonePatches}}` IS the supported call" — would
turn a lucky recovery into a designed one.

### F10 📄 `returnNotes: false` — the documented cure for a documented trap — is rejected by name

**Trying to:** write the six section clips of the flip. The shared `daw-wire-reference`
skill has a whole section headed "**`clip_add_midi` echoes every note back — turn it off

I did exactly that, on a 42-note clip.

```
"clip.addMIDI: unknown parameters 'returnNotes', 'startBeat' — valid keys are
 'atBeat', 'lengthBeats', 'name', 'notes', 'trackId'"
```

Two separate things in one error:

**(a) `returnNotes` does not exist here.** I cannot tell from any document I am allowed
to read whether that is an MCP-layer-only convenience (like `zonePatches` in F9, but in
the opposite direction) or a stale doc. Either way the effect on me was the same: the
documented fix for a documented context-budget trap is unavailable, with no note
anywhere saying which layer it lives on. F9 shows the skills *can* do this well — §7
explicitly names the raw-wire field one layer down. Nothing does that here, so I ate the
full note echo on all six clips.

**(b) `startBeat` vs `atBeat`.** My mistake, but a doc-shaped one. §9's units table lists
"`atBeat`, `startBeat`, `fromBeat`, `lengthBeats`, `gridBeats`" in a single cell against
a tool list that includes `clip_add_midi`, without saying which tool takes which name —
and `startBeat` is simultaneously the correct name for the note-level field *inside*
the same payload. So one call has `atBeat` for the clip and `startBeat` for each note,
and the table gives you no way to know that.

✅ **The error message is, again, excellent** — it names both offending keys *and*
enumerates the complete valid set, so one round-trip fixed both. Consistent with §3a's
"parameters that do not belong … are REJECTED by name, not silently ignored," which is
the right policy and is clearly applied surface-wide.

**Cost:** one failed call, self-correcting. Recorded because (a) is a genuine doc gap
even though (b) was my error.

### F11 📄 `audio.profileFile` has a required parameter that appears in no document

**Trying to:** prove the deliverable WAV on disk is not silence — the task's gate, and

### F12 🐛 A digitally silent window drops `rmsDb` AND `peakDb` instead of reporting silence

Same call, on the successful run. Three of 48 windows came back as **just**
`{"durationSeconds": 1, "startSeconds": 36}` — both measurement keys gone:

```
windows with NO rmsDb key: [36, 46, 47]
full record: [{'durationSeconds': 1, 'startSeconds': 36},
              {'durationSeconds': 1, 'startSeconds': 46},
              {'durationSeconds': 0.8, 'startSeconds': 47}]
```

Presumably `-inf` dB is not JSON-encodable. But the chosen encoding — omit the key —
means **"this second is digital silence" and "this field was not computed" are the same
bytes.** That is the F6 anti-pattern a third time, and it lands on the *one* tool whose
job is to answer "is my render silent?" A naive consumer doing
`min(w['rmsDb'] for w in windows)` gets a `KeyError`; a slightly less naive one doing
`w.get('rmsDb', 0)` reports **0 dB** — the loudest possible value — for pure silence.

**Suggested fix:** emit `"rmsDb": null` plus `"silent": true`, or a floor sentinel like
−200. Anything present-and-explicit.

✅ **That said, this tool is the reason the deliverable got better.** The absent window
at **36 s was a real one-second hole in my own arrangement** — a two-beat gap in the
Break where the 0.333 s chops had decayed and nothing else was sounding. I could not
hear it and no other tool showed it. I filled it (a reversed riser at beat 50 plus a
sub under it), re-rendered, and the hole is gone: 46 of 47 windows now carry signal, the
only silent one being the tail after the music ends. **A per-second profile of the
written file is the single most useful earless instrument in this whole surface.**

### F13 📄 `render.measureLoudness` cannot measure a rendered file — it re-renders the project

The task and the skill both frame the gate as "render, then measure." Natural reading:
measure the thing you rendered. It does not do that.

```
node daw.mjs render.measureLoudness '{"path":"…/sample-flip-final.wav"}'
→ "render.measureLoudness: unknown parameter 'path' — valid keys are
   'durationSeconds', 'fromBeat'"
```

It renders the *project* again over a beat window and meters that. Usually the same
audio — but not the same evidence: it cannot catch a truncated write, a wrong path, a
file that never landed, or a mixdown that used a different window than the one you
metered. The skill's "your ears" list puts `render_measure_loudness` and
`audio_profile_file` side by side as if they were two views of one thing; they are
measurements of two *different objects* (the project vs. the file), and only the second
is evidence about the artifact you are shipping. I ran both, and say which is which
below.

Related, and undocumented: **`render.mixdown` returns no loudness at all**
(`{path, durationSeconds, sampleRate, channels}`), while `render.bounce` returns a full
`report` with `input`/`output` loudness and `appliedGainDb`. Nothing says the two
differ; I only found out by calling both.

### F14 😖 Addressing the master chain is an undocumented magic string

To catch the clipping (below) I wanted a limiter on the master. The wire reference says
> "put a `gain` insert (`gainLinear` 0-16 …) at index 0 of the master chain"

…and never says how to *name* the master to `fx_add`, which takes a `trackId`. The
master is not a track: it does not appear in `project_overview`'s `tracks` array (it is
a separate `master` object with only `{volume}`), so there is no id to capture, and the
id-capture discipline ("never invent, guess, or reconstruct one") gives no way forward.

I guessed `"trackId": "master"`. It worked:
```
node daw.mjs fx.add '{"trackId":"master","kind":"limiter"}'
→ {"effectId":"5189BDC5-…","trackId":"master","effects":[{"kind":"limiter",
   "params":{"ceilingDb":-1,"truePeak":0,"releaseMs":50},"latencySamples":240}]}
```
A working guess is still a guess, and this one is load-bearing for every "make it loud
enough" instruction in the plugin. **One sentence — "the master chain is addressed as
`trackId: \"master\"`" — closes it.**

Two more things nobody told me, both of which change results:
- **`limiter`'s `truePeak` defaults to `0`, i.e. true-peak limiting is OFF.** A ceiling
  named `ceilingDb: -1` that is not true-peak by default will let inter-sample peaks
  through, which is exactly what the loudness meter then reports. I set it to `1`
  (a number, per the reference's "every `fx_set_param` value is a number") and the
  measured true peak locked to −1.000 dBTP.
- **`fx.add` appends**, so a limiter added first sits *before* a gain added second —
  the opposite of what you want. `fx.reorder` fixes it, but its param is `index`, not
  `toIndex`:
  ```
  "fx.reorder: unknown parameter 'toIndex' — valid keys are 'effectId', 'index', 'trackId'"
  ```
  (Good error again. Consistent surface-wide.)

### F15 📄 `divisions: N` yields N−1 pads, and the warning text describes a mode it isn't in

Resampling step. `mode: "divisions"`, `divisions: 8`, on the printed Turn section:

```
node daw.mjs clip.sliceToSampler '{"clipId":"766A9538-…","mode":"divisions","divisions":8,
                                   "baseNote":60,"generateMIDI":false}'
→ {"sliceCount": 7, "headSeconds": 0.7142708333333333, …}
```

**`divisions: 8` → 7 pads.** The skill's table says "`divisions` | 2..128 **equal
parts**", which reads as a promise of exactly N. What actually happens is that the first
1/N of the file becomes the discarded head, so you get N−1 pads and lose the first
eighth of your material unless you pass `includeHead: true` (which I then did, and got
the honest 8). Defensible behaviour inherited from transients mode — but in `divisions`
mode "8 equal parts" that returns 7 and silently drops the downbeat is a genuine
surprise, and it is the *second* time in one session the head default cost me a call.

Compounding it, the warning is written in transients-mode language:
> "0.714 s of audio before the first **slice point** was discarded (pad 1 starts at the
> first **onset**, like an MPC or Recycle) … worth doing **if the detector missed the
> downbeat**"

There is no detector and no onset in `divisions` mode — it runs no detector at all, as
§3b says explicitly. The advice ("if the detector missed the downbeat") is unactionable
here and mildly misleading: in divisions mode the head is not a detector artifact, it is
arithmetic, and it is *always* exactly 1/N.

**Suggested fix:** either default `includeHead: true` for `divisions` mode, or say in
the table that N divisions produce N−1 pads by default; and give the warning a
mode-appropriate sentence.

### F7 + F14 — addendum: the naming split and the master read-back, confirmed

**F7 addendum.** A third name for the same thing: `fx.add` returns effects with
`{kind, id, params, isBypassed, latencySamples}`, but `project.overview` renders the
same effect as `{name, id, bypassed}` — `kind` → **`name`**, `isBypassed` →
**`bypassed`**, and `params` gone entirely. So `[e['kind'] for e in track['fx']]`
raises `KeyError` on the output of the tool you are told to prefer. Same family as the
`isMuted`/`muted` split; same cost.

**F14 addendum — the master chain is effectively write-only on the cheap path.** Having
built it, I could not read it back:

- `project.overview` → `"master": {"volume": 1}`. No effects. It does not appear in
  `tracks` either, so there is nothing to inspect.
- `fx.describe {"trackId": "master"}` →
  `"fx.describe: unknown parameter 'trackId' — valid keys are 'kind'"`.
  That tool describes an effect **type**, not a track's chain — a reasonable tool with a
  name that reads like the one I wanted.
- The only read that works is `project.snapshot`'s **`masterEffects`** — a top-level key
  named nowhere in either skill, on the call both skills spend paragraphs telling you to
  avoid. (Here it was a harmless 53 KB, because this project is small.)

It did confirm the chain landed correctly:
```json
"masterEffects": [
  {"kind":"gain",   "params":{"gainLinear":1.7}, "latencySamples":0},
  {"kind":"limiter","params":{"ceilingDb":-1,"releaseMs":50,"truePeak":1},
   "latencySamples":240}
]
"pdc": {"masterChainLatencySamples":240, "outputLatencySamples":240}
"contentEndBeat": 64
```
✅ and the 240-sample limiter latency is reported as compensated, which is the kind of
thing I would otherwise have had to take on faith.

## The flip — what was actually made

**Source:** `break-90bpm.wav`, 5.683 s, 44.1 kHz stereo. Analyzer: no steady tempo
(honest — the clip is under 6 s), `key.tonal: false`, centroid 1084 Hz, −18.24 dB RMS,
−1.70 dBFS peak.

**Chop:** `mode: "transients"`, `minSliceSeconds: 0.25`, `includeHead: true` →
**16 pads on MIDI 36–51**, one eighth note each, `warnings: []`, head kept as pad 0.
Reconstruction A/B'd against the source at **−17.999 vs −18.345 LUFS** before it was
touched.

**Project moved to 84 BPM** — deliberately fighting the record's 90, per §"Change the
project tempo so the chops fight the grid." The chops keep their recorded speed, so
every eighth note now lands 7 % early against the grid and leaves ~24 ms of air behind
it.

**What makes it a flip and not the loop replayed:**

| Move | How |
|---|---|
| **Reordered** | A new 16-slot pattern: the record's *last-bar* backbeat (pad 50, `strength` 1.000) becomes the *first-bar* backbeat; pad 38 (0.998) moves to beat 5. Nothing plays in source order. |
| **Hits dropped** | The `Turn` and second half of `Main B` run a variant with the beat-0.5, 3.5, 5.5 and 7.5 hits removed. |
| **Stutter** | Pad 50 repeated on four 16ths across beat 7–8 of the variant — the choke group makes each cut the last. |
| **Repitched (16 Levels, down)** | Pad 36's zone widened to **24–36**, so the head/kick chop answers a whole low octave and plays a sub line (notes 24/26/29/31) — same audio, read at 0.50–0.75× rate. |
| **Repitched (16 Levels, up)** | Pad 51's zone widened to **51–63**. |
| **Reversed** | That same pad 51 zone set `reverse: true` — it is the backwards riser into every section and the backwards tail that ends the record. |
| **Choke** | `chokeGroup: 1` on the fourteen drum pads (37–50) so consecutive chops cut each other, **while pad 36 (the sub) and pad 51 (the riser) stay polyphonic and ring through** — the thing §5 says global mono cannot do. |
| **Crushed** | `bitCrusher` at untouched defaults on the chop track: `bits 12, downsampleHz 26040, antiAlias 0, dither 0, mix 1` — the SP-1200 setting, exactly as §6 documents. |
| **Second element** | A resample pass (below), chopped into 8 pads and played back sparsely underneath `Main B` and `Out` at −10.5 dB. |

**Resample loop closed.** `render.bounce` of the `Turn` section (beat 24, 5.72 s, no
`lufsTarget`) → −20.27 LUFS / −2.26 dBTP, `appliedGainDb: 0`. Re-imported with
`clip.addAudio`, re-analyzed (**centroid 340 Hz vs the source's 1084 Hz** — measurably
darker after the sub and the crusher; that is a measurement, not a claim about how it
sounds), then chopped again with `mode: "divisions", divisions: 8, baseNote: 60`. The
second chop reports `sampleRate: 48000` against the first's `44100` — §9's "frames are
not portable across files" made concrete inside one project.

**Arrangement:** 16 bars, 64 beats, 6 sections with markers —
`Intro (0) · Main A (8) · Turn (24) · Main B (32) · Break (48) · Out (56)`. 164 notes.
